1. What **problem** are you solving? 
	1. Visualizing sorting algorithms can be difficult for computer science students. Placing them within a VR setup where they can physically see and interact with the algorithm would help them *feel* the algorithm while it's executing, which can help with their learning.
2. What **context** do we need to understand for your project? For instance, is this for a time far in the future or the immediate future (tomorrow, next year)? 
	1. Immediate future - can be implemented now. The context is really just algorithms, specifically sorting algorithms, which are used to sort a data set (typically an "array" or list) in some predefined order, typically in ascending order by value.
3. Who is your **user**? A user persona may be appropriate for your project, but is not required. 
	1. College computer science students - who want to gain a deeper understanding of algorithms and how to design and analyze them.
4. What do you envision as the **final deliverable** at the end of the project? For instance, a functioning prototype, a figma prototype, a VR prototype made in something like uptale, a physical prototype, a video, a website, etc. 
	1. A functioning Godot VR game that implements a lesson in selection sort - functionality first, pleasant visuals second.
5. What will your project **look** like? Consider structure as well as aesthetics. A mood board or examples of similar projects will help your explain. 
	1. A simple sci-fi room with a control panel (to reset/select algorithm) and a series of blocks in scattered order in slots, where you have to sort them like the sorting algorithm (you're permitted only actions that the sorting algorithm would take).
	2. Example look below (Echo VR)

6. What will define **success** for your project? What are your goals, and how will you know you've achieved them?
	1. One working sorting algorithm experience with a fairly pleasant design. The player will be able to randomize a data set of a size of their choice, and then physically execute the algorithm by comparing and swapping blocks.

	1. Is a functional VR prototype required, or can a low-fidelity prototype demonstrate the proposed experience?
	2. How much weight is placed on the working implementation versus the UX process - research, usability testing, and iteration?
